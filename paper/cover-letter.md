Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “The Hidden Work of AI: Measuring Human Effort and Interaction Cost in Real-World Generative AI Conversations,” as a Research Article.

The manuscript is about people using a generative system, not about a new algorithm. It measures the work users do after an initial request—clarifying, correcting, constraining, restoring a dropped requirement, repeating, revising, verifying, and redirecting—and relates that work to whether the conversation shows resolution, continued repair, or abandonment. The discussion is aimed at conversational interfaces that would have to remember constraints and corrections if that work is to be reduced. That focus sits inside the journal’s scope: people, interaction, and user experience, rather than models considered on their own.

The study is a secondary analysis of the public, opt-in WildChat-1M corpus. It recruits no participants and calls no model API. Human coders apply a reliability-checked scheme to a quota sample of English conversations. A stop after the first reply is treated as indeterminate rather than as success. The paper positions itself against recent accounts of collaboration patterns, implicit feedback, grounding failures, and token-weighted collaborative productivity, and it claims only the distinction those accounts do not make: which kinds of observable human work the interaction imposes, and how those kinds co-occur with behavioral ending states.
